Case Study

U-Shop: a tech marketplace built for Ghanaian students

A consumer-to-consumer marketplace where university students in Ghana can buy and sell tech, designed and documented end to end before and during the build.

Status
V1 in development
My role
Product, design & engineering
Audience
University students, Ghana
Domain
ushopgh.com

The Problem

Students buy and sell laptops, phones and accessories constantly, but they mostly do it through scattered social posts and word of mouth. That makes it hard to find what you need, hard to trust who you are dealing with, and hard to pay safely.

  • No dedicated place: listings are spread across chats and feeds with no consistent structure.
  • Trust: buyers and sellers have little way to confirm who the other person is.
  • Payments: mobile money is how people actually pay in Ghana, so a marketplace has to treat it as a first-class option.

My Approach

I treated U-Shop as a product first and a codebase second. Before writing production code I produced a full documentation set, then moved into building with the live code as the source of truth.

Market research

I benchmarked the product against Jumia Ghana's live platform to understand which marketplace features users already expect, then used that to decide what belongs in V1 and what waits.

Key Technical & Product Decisions

  • Live code over documents: once coding began, I made the codebase the source of truth. Earlier specs had drifted from reality, and keeping two versions of the truth costs more than it saves.
  • Managed auth and content services: the stack moved to Clerk for authentication and Sanity for content, so effort goes into the marketplace itself rather than rebuilding commodity infrastructure.
  • Two payment providers in parallel: Hubtel for Mobile Money and Paystack for card and MoMo, because that is how students actually pay.
  • Defer payments implementation: I postponed the gateway work so the rest of the remediation plan could keep moving without being blocked on integrations.
  • Lightweight student verification: a university email domain check only, with no ID upload in V1, to keep sign-up friction low.
  • Installable PWA: a good fit for a mobile-heavy audience.

Design System

A written design system keeps the interface consistent: the Plus Jakarta Sans typeface, a full color scale, semantic colors for order states, and CSS variables compatible with shadcn/ui. The brand palette is built around red, deep purple and magenta.

Red #FF0000 Deep Purple #5D1A89 Magenta #D1148A

Challenges & Learnings

  • Plans change: the architecture went through several revisions as I learned more, so I recorded decisions and their reasons as I went.
  • Scope discipline: a marketplace has endless features; a written in-scope and out-of-scope list was the best defense against creep.
  • Documentation has a shelf life: treating code as the source of truth after the build started removed a whole class of confusion.
